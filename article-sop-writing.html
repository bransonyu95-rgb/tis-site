Step 1 — Brain Dump the Process

Don't open a Word document. Dump everything you know into a structured bullet list — fill in what you know, leave blanks where you're unsure.

Brain Dump Format
PROCESS NAME: [e.g. "First Article Inspection (FAI) for new part number"] PURPOSE: - Why does this process exist? What goes wrong if it's not done? - [e.g. "To verify that a new or revised part meets drawing requirements before full production run"] TRIGGER / WHEN TO DO THIS: - When does this process start? What event triggers it? - [e.g. "Triggered when: new part number approved, drawing revision, machine transfer, or 90-day gap in production"] INPUTS (what you need before starting): - [e.g. "Approved engineering drawing (latest revision)"] - [e.g. "Customer-approved inspection plan, CMM program, First-off part from production"] THE STEPS (rough, in order — don't worry about formatting): - [Step 1: what to do] - [Step 2: what to do] - [continue through all steps] CRITICAL CHECKS (what can go wrong, what to verify): - [e.g. "Confirm drawing revision level matches CMM program revision"] - [e.g. "Check GD&T callouts — positional tolerance requires CMM, not manual measurement"] OUTPUTS (what comes out of this process): - [e.g. "Completed FAI report", "Signed-off sample part tagged and stored"] REFERENCES: - [ISO standard, work instruction number, customer spec, etc.]

Step 2 — Choose the Right SOP Format

Rule of thumb: audited procedures need ISO format, floor tasks need a Work Instruction, machine-side reminders need a QRG.

Use when: Audit-required procedures

ISO 9001 Standard SOP

Part of your QMS, subject to audit, or required by a customer standard (IATF 16949, AS9100, ISO 13485). Structure: Purpose → Scope → Responsibilities → Procedure → References → Revision history.

Use when: Production floor tasks

Work Instruction (WI)

For specific tasks performed by operators, clear enough for a new hire to follow. Structure: tools and materials → safety → step-by-step instructions → acceptance criteria. Tone: direct imperative — "Insert the workpiece," not "The workpiece should be inserted."

Use when: Visual reminders for experienced operators

Quick Reference Guide (QRG)

For experienced operators who need a visual reminder, not a full procedure. One page or a laminated card — main parameters, pass/fail criteria, minimal text.

Step 3 — Build the AI Prompt

Copy this prompt, fill in your brain dump from Step 1, and paste it into your AI tool of choice.

AI Prompt — SOP Draft
You are a Quality Systems Engineer with 10 years of experience writing manufacturing SOPs for ISO 9001-certified contract manufacturers. You write procedures that are technically accurate, audit-ready, and clear enough for operators to follow without supervision. I need you to write a complete [ISO 9001 SOP / Work Instruction / Quick Reference Guide] for the following process. FORMAT: Use [ISO 9001 format with numbered sections including Purpose, Scope, Definitions, Responsibilities, Procedure, References, Revision History / Work Instruction format with safety first, numbered steps, acceptance criteria / QRG one-page table format]. TONE: [Formal and procedural (for ISO SOP) / Direct imperative — "Do this, then do that" (for Work Instruction) / Minimal text, table-based (for QRG)] PROCESS INFORMATION: [PASTE YOUR BRAIN DUMP FROM STEP 1 HERE] ADDITIONAL REQUIREMENTS: - Include a document number placeholder: [DOC-XXX] - Include a revision field: Rev 01 | Date: [DATE] - Include a document owner field: [DEPARTMENT / ROLE] - Flag any step where you need more information from me - If any step has a common error or safety risk, add a NOTE or WARNING callout Write the complete document. No placeholders left unfilled except document number, date, and owner fields.

Read the full draft before asking for changes, one section at a time.

Step 4 — Review for Accuracy

AI will produce a clean, well-structured SOP — and make assumptions. Replace every generic tolerance, machine reference, or inspection frequency with your actual numbers.

Compliance Check — If This SOP Needs to Pass an Audit

  • Scope accurately describes what this SOP covers, and what it doesn't
  • Responsibilities are assigned to roles, not people
  • References cite the correct standard numbers and revisions
  • Regulatory requirements (REACH, RoHS, customer flow-down) are called out
  • The procedure links to related forms or records

Floor Reality Check

Take the draft to whoever does the job. Watch where they hesitate or add a step from memory. Those gaps are the SOP's gaps.

After three SOPs this way, what used to take 3–4 hours takes 25–35 minutes. Post the finished document where the work happens, and revisit it within 60 days — an SOP nobody revisits is a compliance risk.

The thinking behind it

Before you write the SOP, ask what to remove

In a 2021 study published in Nature, Gabrielle Adams and colleagues found that people improving a design almost never considered removing pieces — until a short cue reminded them removal was an option, which roughly doubled subtractive answers. A French replication (Fillon and colleagues, 2025) confirmed it: asked “what should we change?”, people generated about four times more additions than removals — and a remove-only question fixed it.

So before AI drafts your SOP, run one extra prompt on the process itself: “Which steps could be removed entirely?” Ask it as its own question, not mixed into a general review — that separation is exactly what the research supports. The best SOP is often a shorter process, written down.
