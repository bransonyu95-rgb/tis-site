The AI Knowledge Gold Rush: How Skills and Courses Are Being Monetised

The Industrial Scribe | AI Trends  ·  2 min read  ·  March 2026

In 2024, the money was in building AI models. In 2025, it moved to AI applications. In 2026, the fastest-growing segment is neither. It's selling the know-how.

A new creator economy has emerged around AI knowledge. The people earning the most aren't engineers. They're domain experts who figured out how to use AI in their own field, then packaged that knowledge to sell.

Anthropic's MCP ecosystem now lists over 19,000 servers. Claude Skills marketplaces host 7,000+ installable skills. But almost all of it is generic — "data analysis," "prompt engineering." Manufacturing is one of the largest verticals with almost zero coverage.

Most AI creators work in tech, marketing, or finance. They've never set foot on a factory floor. They don't know what WIP data looks like, or why an advance payment proposal needs a specific structure. The people with that experience haven't yet realised they're sitting on a monetisable asset.

Generic content is crowded and cheap. Vertical-specific knowledge is scarce and expensive.

A generic "data analysis prompt pack" competes with thousands of free alternatives and sells for $12. A manufacturing-specific skill bundle that knows what a Preclosure row is has zero competition. It can command $39–49. B2B audiences show 3–5x higher price tolerance because the ROI is measurable.

Fewer than 10 dedicated AI skills or courses exist globally for manufacturing, and zero target ASEAN manufacturers specifically. The market isn't asking "Is there demand?" 98% of manufacturers exploring AI already answered that. The market is asking "Who will be the trusted source?" That position is still open.
