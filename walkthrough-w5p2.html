Before the demo: a 1-minute lesson

AI only knows what you tell it

Most people open ChatGPT, type a question, and get an answer written for nobody in particular. The AI is clever, but it has never met your boss. It doesn't know who will read the answer, so it guesses.

To get drafts you can actually send, AI needs four things about your work:

1Who you areYour job, your company, the work you redo every week.Mei Ling: production planner, food-packaging printer.
2Who reads your workYour boss, your customer, your supplier, and what each one cares about.Mr. Tan, ops manager: output vs plan.
3How they like itShort or detailed, numbers first, the format they expect.Short. He hates long emails.
4Your red linesNames to hide, things never to promise, what must never go out.Never name customers in internal documents.

Write these four things down once, in a plain text file (an .md file), and the AI reads them before every answer. Now it thinks about your reader for you. You stop re-explaining, stop rewriting, and your brain gets a break.

This is the most basic way to use AI at work. It's also the foundation everything else stands on.
