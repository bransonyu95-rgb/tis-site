The moment this is for

Something eats your hours every week. A changeover that takes forever. A drawing change that sits for days. An order that takes two weeks to ship but only two hours to make. A report you rebuild by hand. You have heard "use AI". But if you automate it as it is, you just get a faster mess.

Use it anywhere in the factory
  • Shop floor. A machine, a cell or a store: changeover time, walking trips, parts waiting, setup scrap.
  • Engineering. Drawings, programs and changes: which version is live, how long a change waits, who keeps getting asked.
  • Process flow. One real order from enquiry to payment: work time against waiting time, and every hand-over.
  • Office. Reports, data entry and chasing: what is copied, retyped and redone.

What it does, in order
  1. Your factory first. A short read of your business nature, so it knows where waste costs your kind of factory most.
  2. Go and see. It asks what really happens, one step at a time, until every step has a time and a reason. It checks your SOP against what people actually do.
  3. The waste map. Every step tagged with the lean 8 wastes, with hours per month from your own numbers. For an order, it shows the plain truth: "about 2 hours of real work in 9 days".
  4. The 5S check, scored out of 25. For a bench, a rack or a folder: Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain. Send a photo and it scores what it can see.
  5. The real cause. The 5 Whys on the biggest waste, until it reaches something you can change.
  6. Cut before you automate. Remove, combine, reorder, simplify. Many hunts save hours with no tool at all.
  7. The new standard. A one-page standard and a simple visual check, so it stays lean.
  8. Automate only what is left. Each step gets a level, from "fix the template" to "an AI skill that runs on your files".
  9. Your one-page Lean Plan. The problem, the waste, the cause, what already worked in your own factory, the target, who does what by when, and the date you check again.

Lessons from inside a real factory

Every plan can also draw on 16 lessons from improvement work inside a real factory. A few of them:

  • Lead time is mostly waiting. Measure the wait, not just the work.
  • Split "planned to wait" from "forgotten" before you blame the floor.
  • The material kit is its own wait.
  • Capture output at the machine, with almost no typing.
  • Remove what nobody uses, even if you built it.
  • A problem needs an owner before it needs a system.

They are patterns, with no company names and no figures. It uses one only when it fits what you found, and it never presents a lesson as your own result.

The honest limits

  • It uses only your numbers. Hours are estimates from your answers until you time the before and after.
  • The AI cannot see your floor. You walk it and answer, or send photos. It does not replace a proper time study.
  • It is a structured AI guide built on lean methods, not a specially trained model. It runs inside the AI tool you already use.
  • Changes to safety (guards, lifting, chemicals, electrical, layout) need your safety officer's OK. Cutting inspection or traceability steps needs your quality owner's OK.
  • Follow your company's rules on putting work information and photos into AI tools. Placeholders like "Customer A" or "machine 3" work just as well.
