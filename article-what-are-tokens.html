What Are Tokens? How AI Reads Your Data and What It Costs

What is a Token?

A token is the unit of text an LLM reads and writes — roughly 3/4 of a word in English. So 100 words is about 130–140 tokens, and a 10-page report is roughly 4,000–5,000 tokens. Think of tokens as the AI's work envelope, like the XYZ range on a CNC machine: a part that fits gets machined perfectly, one that exceeds it gets rejected or needs multiple setups. If your data exceeds the token limit, the AI truncates it or you split the work into sessions.

Why Tokens Matter

Every AI model has a context window — the total text it can hold in one conversation, including your input and its replies.

ModelContext WindowRoughly Equivalent To
GPT-4o128,000 tokens~200 pages of text
Claude Sonnet200,000 tokens~300 pages of text
Claude with extended context1,000,000 tokens~1,500 pages of text

When a BD manager uploads 334,000 rows of WIP data into Claude, the large context window holds it all at once. A smaller window would force the AI to process it in chunks, losing the ability to cross-reference the full dataset.

If you use AI through an API or a tool like Claude Code, you pay per token — input and output priced separately, so a big upload or a long report costs more than a short question. Long conversations also cost quality: older tokens get pushed out of the window, so experienced users start a fresh conversation for big new tasks.

  • One page of text ≈ 500 tokens
  • A 10,000-row spreadsheet ≈ 500,000–800,000 tokens
  • Break large files into logical chunks — by customer, by year — if you hit limits
  • Start big new analysis in a fresh conversation, not a 2-hour-old thread

The Bottom Line

Tokens are how AI measures text — what you send and what you get back. More tokens means more room to work with, but also more cost. On a $20/month plan, most manufacturing tasks won't hit the limit. Uploading massive datasets is where it starts to matter.
