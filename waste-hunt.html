The Waste Hunt.
The lean way to find the waste, then automate what's left.

What it does, in order

  1. Your factory first. A short read of your business nature: commodity volume, high-mix custom, qualified precision, plus "captive" if one or two customers are most of your revenue. This says where waste costs your kind of factory most.
  2. One real task, step by step. It asks for your written SOP, or notes that there is none, then keeps asking until it knows what really happens: who, which system, what is copied from where to where, how many minutes, how often, who waits, what gets redone. It digs for the hidden steps: searching, chasing, retyping, reformatting, waiting for a signature.
  3. The waste map. Every step tagged with the office version of the lean 8 wastes (waiting, moving things around, pile-up, searching, extra processing, making what nobody uses, mistakes and rework, wasted talent), with hours per month from your own numbers.
  4. The 5S check. The files, folders, reports and spreadsheets behind the task, scored on Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize and Sustain, out of 25, with one clear action for each S.
  5. Cut before you automate. Eliminate, combine, rearrange or simplify first. Some hunts save hours with no tool at all.
  6. What to automate, and how far. Each remaining step gets a level, from "fix the template" to "an AI skill that runs on your files". The top three are ranked by hours, ease and risk.
  7. A build guide for number one. What to prepare, the exact prompt or build steps, a test on one real case, before-and-after timing, the updated SOP, and a short regular check so it stays lean.

5S, for office work

On the shop floor, 5S keeps a workstation clear. The Hunt applies the same five to the workspace behind your task: the files, folders, reports, spreadsheets and inbox.

  • Sort. What does nobody need? Old file versions, report columns nobody reads, whole reports nobody opens, extra people on copy, "just in case" steps.
  • Set in order. One place for everything. Could a colleague covering for you find the current file in 30 seconds: one folder, one naming rule, the latest version obvious?
  • Shine. Is the source clean? Duplicates, wrong or missing entries, broken formulas and links, unread pile-ups. Are problems spotted while working, or found later?
  • Standardize. Is the agreed way written down, as an SOP, a template, a checklist or a naming rule, so anyone does it the same?
  • Sustain. Is there an owner, and a short regular check that stops it sliding back?

Each S gets a score from your own answers and files, a finding and one action. Sort and Set in order feed the cut list. Standardize and Sustain feed the updated SOP. Re-score it a month later to see if it held.

The hard rule: anything that leaves or commits the company (sending, approving, ordering, pricing, signing) stays a human click. AI drafts, a person decides. Anything that connects to company systems needs IT's OK first.

Two ways to run it

A · No install, any AI chat

Open paste-prompt.md, copy the box into a new chat in ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, free plans included, and answer one question at a time. About 30 minutes.

B · Claude Code skill

For people who already use Claude Code (needs Claude Pro, USD 20/month). Move the folder to your skills folder, drop your SOP, a sample report or an export into sop-in/, and say waste hunt. It reads your files, does the sums with a script, saves every hunt, and keeps a log of hours found and hours actually saved. If you want, it then builds number one with you and tests it on your real file.

The honest limits

  • It uses only your numbers. Hours saved and the 5S score come from your answers until you time the before and after.
  • It is a structured AI interview built on lean methods, not a specially trained model. It runs inside the AI chat you already use.
  • It finds waste in office workflows: reports, data entry, chasing, approvals, documents. It is not a shop-floor time study.
  • Follow your company's rules on putting work information into AI tools. Placeholders like "Customer A" work just as well.
