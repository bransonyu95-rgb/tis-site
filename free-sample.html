What business are you really in?

The first step of Module 01 — free, usable today

Two shops with identical machines can be opposite businesses. One runs 10,000 identical brackets for a trader. The other runs 12-piece batches for a semiconductor tier-1. Same machines. Different rules for pricing, reports, everything.

Before you copy anyone's template, answer six questions. Honestly. They tell you which business you actually run.

The six questions

  1. What do you make or supply, and for which sectors?
  2. What is your volume pattern — one-off custom, repeat batches, or high-volume runs?
  3. Who is your typical customer — end OEM, tier-1, or a trader?
  4. How do orders arrive — competitive enquiry each time, rolling POs, or annual agreements?
  5. How many other companies nearby can realistically do your typical job?
  6. What does it take for a new customer to start buying from you — a PO, sample approval, or a full audit?

Your answers point to one of three business natures — A, B, or C — plus one overlay condition that changes your risk completely.
