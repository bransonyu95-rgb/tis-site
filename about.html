My story, in 30 seconds

The people next to me taught me four things.

1

Only make what someone asks for.

Every screen I added started with a real question from someone.

2

Use their own files.

The planner's seven views were built from the planner's own sheets.

3

Hide a number nobody trusts.

One target showed zero against seventy. I switched it off the first time.

4

Nobody uses it? Take it down.

I built a wall board. It looked right. Nobody used it. It is gone.

And my own rules

  • Only publish what I've run on real work
  • Real numbers, nothing invented
  • No customer names, ever
  • No jargon, mamak-table English only
  • For office staff, never for programmers

Where my work goes

High-tech equipment

I work on the supply side of high-tech equipment: precision parts and modules for machines where "roughly correct" does not exist. At that tier, a wrong number in a report costs real money.

And beyond

The same precision work supports production lines in other regulated industries, places where the numbers have to be right the first time.

If my insight survives at that tier, it will survive your office.

What AI changed for me

Nobody taught me this. I'm not from the technical side. I present, I plan, I do content and strategy. But with a framework and AI, I produce work from fields I never studied.
