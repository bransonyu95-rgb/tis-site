Everyone Is 'Exploring' AI. Almost Nobody Is Ready. That's Your Opening.

The Industrial Scribe | AI Trends  ·  2 min read  ·  March 2026

Ask any factory boss whether AI matters and you'll get a yes — 98% of manufacturers say they're exploring it. Then ask whether the company is actually ready to use it. Only 20% can say yes with a straight face.

Most of the other 80% are stuck in the pilot loop: a proof of concept, an internal presentation, a steering committee, another proof of concept. Nothing ever reaches the floor.

Where the gap actually lives

Here's the surprise in the survey data: cost barely registers. The real barriers are softer things.

The five real barriers (% of manufacturers)
Insufficient understanding of AI — people don't know what it can actually do
47%
Lack of talent / skills — no one internally knows how to start
42%
Data infrastructure not ready — ERP exports messy, siloed, inconsistent
38%
Unclear ROI — management won't fund what it can't measure
35%
Risk & governance concerns — legal and compliance are cautious
29%
Sorted big-to-small, one accent colour on the vital bar — the TIS way.

Look at what's not on that chart: money. A Claude or ChatGPT subscription costs less than one hour of CNC machine time. The blockage is that nobody knows where to start — and the people who'd benefit most, the BD managers and QA engineers and planners, are not the ones being sent for training.

The lunch-break economy

Southeast Asia's numbers look contradictory until you spot the pattern. A McKinsey–EDB study puts 46% of SEA companies past the pilot stage, ahead of the global 35%. Yet in Singapore, 75% of individual employees use AI — while only 15% of SMEs have adopted it at company level.

Translation: the adoption is happening at lunch break. The BD manager uploads Excel to ChatGPT between meetings. The supervisor drafts an SOP on a personal phone at the mamak. Officially, the company has no AI. Unofficially, it runs on it every day.

Why I call this good news

Because the gap between companies is really a gap between individuals — and an individual can move this quarter, without waiting for the IT department's blessing. Nothing stops you becoming the person in your office who turns a day of quote preparation into an hour.

In 2024, being in the 80% was normal. In 2026, it's a queue. The people walking past the queue are the ones who trained themselves.
