The method, in everything it does

Before any chart, three questions. Who is in the room, and what do they already believe? What is the one thing the data proves? That sentence becomes the title: not "Delivery Performance Q3" but "On-time delivery recovered to 96% after the layout change". So what must happen next? The decision, approval or action the slide exists to trigger.

Three ways to use it

  1. Deck. The one message, 3 to 5 slides with takeaway titles, the right chart for each and what to delete, the ask with a date, and the number they will challenge, with your backup answer ready.
  2. The so-what dashboard. A normal dashboard shows numbers and leaves people to work out what they mean. This one tells them: an ACT TODAY band at the top with at most three things that crossed your action rules, each with what to do, who does it and by when. Every tile titled with its takeaway. Every tile with an action line. Grey by default, colour only where action is needed. Measures nobody acts on moved to the bottom and flagged honestly.
  3. Check. Paste or describe a chart or slide you already made. Get the takeaway title, what to delete, and the fix.

Every result ends with the 10-point pre-ship check, so you know it is ready before the meeting.

The honest limits

  • It uses only your numbers. It will not invent targets, thresholds or owners. If your dashboard has none, it lists what is missing, because without them a dashboard cannot tell anyone to act.
  • It finds what the data proves. It will not invent a cause the data does not show.
  • Follow your company's rules on putting work data into AI tools. Placeholders for customer names and money figures work just as well.
