The moment this is for

You have a good idea: a new machine, one more planner, a better process, a price increase the customer must accept. You spend two days on the slides. The GM says "okay, noted" and nothing happens. The idea was fine. The ask was not built for the person who decides.

What it does

  1. Maps who really decides. Who signs, who can quietly block it, who must live with it, and what each one gains, fears and believes today.
  2. Does the money maths from your numbers. Payback, return, the cost of waiting, break-even, with every assumption in one table and a conservative case, so the finance manager cannot tear it apart. Missing figures are marked [CHECK], never invented.
  3. Answers the six approver questions before they are asked. Why this, why now, why this option, how much and when do we get it back, what could go wrong, what exactly do you need from me.
  4. Builds the options. Do nothing, a small step, the full fix, with one recommended and a stop point that makes the yes feel safe.
  5. Prepares you for the room. The 5 hardest questions with short answers, and the number most likely to be challenged.
  6. Writes it. A one-page memo, an email or a short deck, with titles that say what to conclude, not what the slide is about.
  7. Rehearses with you. It plays your GM or your customer, asks the hard questions, then tightens your answers.

Nine ready layouts

Investment proposal · business plan · budget or headcount ask · new process or system · customer price increase · recovery plan · monthly management review · customer business review · quality update.

The honest limits

  • It uses only your numbers. It will not invent a saving, a target, an owner or someone's opinion, and it will tell you if your case does not add up.
  • It makes your case clearer and harder to argue with. It cannot make a bad idea good, and the decision is still the decider's.
  • Tested in role-play runs, not with paying customers yet. Follow your company's rules on putting work data into AI tools; placeholders for names and money figures work just as well.
