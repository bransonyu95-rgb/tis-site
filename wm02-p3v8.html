Present Like the Insider

Bookmark this page — it's yours, forever. Come back before every big meeting.

In this module: The So-What Method · Chart-choice map · The declutter pass · 🤖 The four prompts · Worked example · By business nature · ✅ Pre-ship checklist · Offline PDF

The core idea: nobody in a factory gets paid for making charts. You get paid for making decisions happen. The difference between a report that gets ignored and one that gets approved is almost never the data — it is whether you knew what the data was supposed to make the audience DO.

Part 1 — The So-What Method

Before any chart, any slide, any AI prompt — answer three questions in one sentence each:

  1. WHO is in the room, and what do they already believe? (Boss thinks delivery is fine · customer suspects quality is slipping · auditor wants proof of control)
  2. WHAT is the one thing the data proves? One thing. Three things = three slides.
  3. SO WHAT must happen next? The decision, the approval, the order — the action this presentation exists to trigger.

Then the rule that changes everything: the answer to question 2 becomes your slide title. Try it:

The default nobody teaches: when in doubt, a sorted bar chart with a takeaway title beats everything fancy. Fancy impresses designers; sorted bars win approvals.

Part 3 — The declutter pass (60 seconds per chart)

  • Delete: 3D effects, background shading, heavy gridlines, borders, the legend if you can label lines directly
  • One accent colour for the thing that matters; everything else grey. Your audience's eye goes where the colour is — use that on purpose
  • Round the numbers: 96%, not 96.37%. Precision the audience can't act on is noise
  • The squint test: blur your eyes. If the main point doesn't survive, the chart isn't done

Part 4 — The four AI prompts

Click copy, paste into ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini, fill the brackets. If you own Module 01, paste your context line on top — every output gets sharper.

Prompt 1 — Data → Story (run this first, every time)

You are a manufacturing presentation coach. I will paste raw data and tell you who the audience is and what I need them to decide. Your job is NOT to summarise the data — it is to find the one message that drives the decision. AUDIENCE: [e.g. my GM, monthly ops meeting / customer quality manager, complaint review] DECISION I NEED: [e.g. approve overtime budget / accept our corrective action and keep the business] DATA: [paste your table here] GIVE ME: 1. The ONE key message, written as a slide title that states the takeaway (max 12 words) 2. The best chart for that message and why (bar / line / Pareto / stacked / big number) 3. What to leave OUT of this presentation and why 4. The one number the audience will challenge — and the backup answer I should prepare 5. A 3-sentence speaker note: what I say when this slide is on screen RULES: No jargon. If the data does not actually support a clear message, say so — do not invent one.

Prompt 2 — The Management Update (Situation → Change → So What → Ask)

Build me a management update presentation from my notes. Audience: [boss / management meeting]. Use exactly this structure, one slide each: 1. SITUATION — where we stand, one takeaway title + max 3 numbers 2. WHAT CHANGED — what moved since last update, good and bad, no hiding 3. SO WHAT — what it means for cost, delivery, or customers, in money or days where possible 4. THE ASK — the decision or support I need, stated as one sentence with a deadline MY NOTES: [brain-dump here — messy is fine] For each slide give me: the takeaway title, the content (bullets or a chart suggestion from my data), and a 2-sentence speaker note. Keep every slide answerable in 60 seconds — this is a factory meeting, not a conference.

Prompt 3 — The Customer-Facing Deck

Build a customer-facing presentation. Formal, confident, professional Asian B2B English — no flowery language, no apologising beyond one sincere line if this is a complaint response. TYPE: [business review / corrective action presentation / capability introduction] CUSTOMER CONTEXT: [who they are, what they care about, what happened] MY MATERIAL: [paste data, findings, actions taken] STRUCTURE it as: (1) purpose in one line, (2) the facts with takeaway titles, (3) actions and evidence, (4) commitment and next steps with dates. RULES: Every claim needs a number or a date. Never blame individuals — processes fail, people don't. Never promise what I did not tell you we can do. End with what we need FROM the customer, if anything.

Prompt 4 — The Chart Doctor (before anything ships)

You are a ruthless chart reviewer. I will describe or screenshot a chart I made. Critique it in this order: 1. TITLE — does it state the takeaway, or just name the topic? 2. MESSAGE — can you tell in 5 seconds what I want the audience to conclude? If not, why? 3. CHART TYPE — is there a simpler chart that says it better? 4. CLUTTER — list everything I should delete 5. THE FIX — describe the corrected chart in one paragraph Be direct. If the chart should be a sentence instead ("just say the number"), tell me. MY CHART: [describe or paste]

Part 5 — Worked example (before / after)

Before: "Monthly Quality Report" — a 14-column defect table, reasons listed alphabetically, three pie charts, title "Quality Data September". Boss reaction: "okay, noted." Nothing happens.

After the method: One Pareto chart, sorted. Title: "Two causes make 71% of our defects — both on Machine 7." One ask: "Approve 4 hours downtime for Machine 7 alignment this Saturday." Approved in the meeting. (Illustrative example — anonymised.)
