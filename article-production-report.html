The 12 Numbers Every Production Manager Should Track Every Week

Section 1 — Production Performance

Data Point 1

Output vs Target

Planned output vs actual output vs variance. If negative, give a one-sentence reason — e.g. a CNC shop planning 3,200 parts that shipped 2,850 needs to say why (M/C 04 down 14 hours) before the customer asks.

Data Point 2

OEE — Overall Equipment Effectiveness

OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality. World-class benchmark: 85%. It tells you whether the loss is a machine, speed, or quality problem.

Data Point 3

Downtime — Planned vs Unplanned

Planned downtime vs unplanned, top cause, and whether a downtime report was raised. Unplanned downtime above 5% of planned time is a warning sign.

Section 2 — Quality Metrics

Data Point 4

Rejection Rate

Total parts made, rejected internally, and customer returns, tracked separately. Target: zero customer complaints a week.

Data Point 5

NCR Count and Status

NCRs opened, closed, open, and overdue. Anything past 14 days almost always becomes a customer escalation.

Data Point 6

CAPA Status

CAPAs open, due this week, and verified effective. "Implemented but not verified" still counts as open.

Section 3 — Inventory & WIP

Data Point 7

WIP Carry-Forward

Jobs in progress, and which ones are aging past 7 days. WIP stuck over 10 days usually hides a bottleneck.

Data Point 8

Material Shortages

Jobs on hold for missing material, and expected arrival. If this section is never empty, the real problem is your PO lead time.

Data Point 9

Aging Jobs

Jobs stuck on the floor past your threshold, the reason, and who's acting on it by when.

Section 4 — People & Capacity

Data Point 10

Headcount vs Required

Required headcount vs actual, and any critical skill gaps (e.g. only one operator certified for M/C 09).

Data Point 11

Overtime This Week

OT hours worked and where. Recurring OT on the same machine is a capacity problem, not a workload spike.

Data Point 12

Upcoming Leave

Planned leave next week, flagged for any critical operator, with a backup plan confirmed.

Section 5 — Forward Look

Delivery commitments at risk, planned maintenance or tooling changes, new jobs needing FAI, and any machine at risk of unplanned downtime — surfaced now, not after it hits the customer.

The thinking behind it

Why 12 numbers beat 40

Working memory holds only about four chunks at a time (Nelson Cowan's landmark 2001 review — the famous “seven” is what you manage after grouping things). In Chase and Simon's 1973 chess experiments, masters recalled about 16 pieces from a real position while beginners managed 4 — but scatter the pieces randomly and the masters' advantage collapses almost entirely. Expertise is a library of patterns, not a bigger brain.

That is why this report works: 12 numbers grouped into meaningful chunks a manager can actually hold and act on. A 40-row data dump is not more informative — it is invisible.
